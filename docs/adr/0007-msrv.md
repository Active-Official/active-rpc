# ADR-0007 - Minimum supported Rust version 1.88

**Status:** accepted (2026-10-27). Supersedes point 4 of [ADR-0006](0006-versioning.md).

## Context

ADR-0006 set the MSRV at 1.85, matching Active Infinity and the Active workspace.
That held while the dependency graph was small. The QUIC transport is the part
that moves: `quinn` → `rcgen` → `time`, and `time` is on the security treadmill.

Two facts forced a decision:

1. `time` 0.3.47 patched RUSTSEC-2026-0009 (stack exhaustion while parsing
   RFC 2822 dates) and requires rustc 1.88. The newest 1.85-compatible release,
   0.3.44, is the vulnerable one.
2. `proptest` 1.12, used by the property tests, also requires rustc 1.88.

So a 1.85 build either pins a vulnerable `time` or fails `cargo deny`. Pinning a
vulnerable crate to keep an old MSRV trades a real advisory for a compatibility
promise that no consumer asked for.

## Decision

1. **The RPC crate's MSRV is 1.88**, declared in `rust-version` and checked in CI
   on 1.88.0 with `--locked --all-targets --all-features`.
2. **No security-relevant pins.** `Cargo.lock` tracks the newest releases;
   `cargo deny check` must pass with no advisory ignores.
3. **Active Infinity stays at 1.85.** Its graph has no `time` and no advisory
   pressure; the wire crate keeps the lower floor.
4. **The lockfile is the tested graph.** CI runs with `--locked` so a resolution
   change is a reviewed diff, not a surprise in a release build.

## Consequences

- A consumer of `active-rpc` needs rustc 1.88 or newer; one of `active-infinity`
  alone still needs only 1.85. The two crates document their floors separately.
- The release workflow must use 1.88+; the release machine has 1.85 through
  1.99 installed, so 1.88 is part of the standard toolchain set.
- Future MSRV moves are ADRs of their own; this one records the reasoning, not
  just the number.
